If you take a sphere, and rotate and reflect it
any way about the origin, you get the same sphere.
Thus all the rotation angles and
all the reflection lines are point of symmetries for the sphere.
So the group of symmetries of the sphere is \(O_3(\mathbb{R})\),
where \(O_3(\mathbb{R})\) is the orthogonal group of 3 x 3 matrices
over the reals \(\mathbb{R}\), satisfying \(O^T = O^{-1}\)
for all matrices in this group.  

Now what about 3D polyhedrons. There are many of them,
infact infinitely many, so now the question arises
what are the symmetry groups of these?
We have one restriction right now.
The symmetries of any polyhedron about the origin
must also be a symmetry of the sphere,
so all these groups are actually subgroups of \(O_3(\mathbb{R})\). 
How many more restrictions can we put?
Probably one that these symmetry groups are finite, 
since polyhedrons can only be rotated and reflected
in finite ways to keep them looking the same,
we now have two conditions.
Finite subgroups of \(O_3(\mathbb{R})\)
